# Use Case — Country HR Manager

> Fictional example for demonstration only.

## Target Role
Country HR Manager for a fast-growing European logistics business.

### Key Requirements
- 8+ years of HR experience
- Strong local employment-law and employee-relations experience
- End-to-end recruiting capability
- Payroll and HR operations oversight
- Experience managing a local HR team
- Ability to partner with a country GM and regional HR leadership
- Fluent business English; local language preferred

## Candidate Summary
- 11 years of HR experience, including 5 years in-country
- Current HR Manager for a 220-employee business
- Leads a team of 3 HR staff
- Owns recruiting, employee relations, payroll vendor coordination, performance cycles, and local HR compliance
- Has supported two restructurings and one works-council consultation process
- Business English fluent; local language native

## Example Evaluation

### Overall Recommendation
- **Fit Score:** 88/100
- **Decision:** Strong Advance
- **Rationale:** Strong local HR ownership, relevant scale, and credible employee-relations depth with clear evidence of team leadership and stakeholder management.

### Hard Gate Check
| Requirement | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 8+ years HR experience | Met | 11 years total HR experience |
| Local employment-law / ER depth | Met | Owns ER cases, restructuring support, works-council consultation |
| Payroll / HR operations | Met | Coordinates payroll vendor and local HR operations |
| Team leadership | Met | Leads 3-person HR team |
| Senior stakeholder partnership | Met | Partners with country GM and regional HR |
| Business English | Met | Fluent |

### Evidence Highlights
- **E3:** Current ownership of HR for 220 employees plus a 3-person HR team
- **E3:** Two restructuring projects and a works-council consultation process
- **E2:** Payroll vendor oversight is clear, but hands-on payroll depth is less explicit

### Strengths
1. Strong country-level HR ownership
2. Relevant employee-relations and restructuring exposure
3. Demonstrated team leadership
4. Good balance of strategic partnering and operational delivery

### Risks / Verification Points
- Verify the complexity of employee-relations cases personally led vs. supported by external counsel
- Confirm depth of compensation and benefits ownership
- Clarify experience in high-volume frontline recruiting if the target business is operationally intensive

### Interview Focus
1. Difficult employee-relations cases and decision ownership
2. Payroll governance and error escalation
3. Managing local vs. regional HR priorities
4. Building HR infrastructure in a scaling environment

### Targeted Interview Questions
1. Tell me about the most complex employee-relations case you personally led. What was your role and outcome?
2. Describe a restructuring you supported. What decisions did you own, and what legal or employee-representative risks did you manage?
3. How do you govern payroll quality when payroll is outsourced?
4. Give an example of a disagreement with a country GM on a people decision. How did you handle it?
5. What HR processes did you build or materially improve in your current company?
6. How large was your annual hiring volume, and which roles were hardest to fill?

### Final Hiring View
Proceed to the next round. The candidate appears strong for a country HR leadership role; the main interview priority is validating the depth of legal/ER ownership and high-volume operational HR exposure.
