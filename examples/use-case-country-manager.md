# Use Case — Country Manager

> Fictional example for demonstration only.

## Target Role
Country Manager for a growing cross-border logistics company.

### Key Requirements
- 10+ years of relevant commercial or operations leadership
- Full-country P&L ownership or equivalent business ownership
- Experience leading multi-function teams
- Track record of growth, customer development, and operational execution
- Strong cross-cultural stakeholder management
- Ability to build local organization and operating cadence

## Candidate Summary
- 14 years in logistics and e-commerce
- Current country lead for a business with €42M annual revenue
- Owns sales, operations, customer success, and local support functions
- Leads 9 direct reports and approximately 310 employees
- Grew revenue by 28% over two years while improving EBITDA margin by 3.2 percentage points
- Opened two new sites and renegotiated three major customer contracts
- Reports to regional CEO

## Example Evaluation

### Overall Recommendation
- **Fit Score:** 93/100
- **Decision:** Strong Advance
- **Rationale:** Strong evidence of country-level business ownership, measurable growth, P&L improvement, organization leadership, and execution across both commercial and operational dimensions.

### Hard Gate Check
| Requirement | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ years relevant leadership | Met | 14 years in logistics / e-commerce |
| Country-level business ownership | Met | Current country lead with multi-function scope |
| P&L responsibility | Met | €42M revenue; EBITDA margin improvement |
| Multi-function leadership | Met | Sales, operations, customer success, support functions |
| Growth track record | Met | Revenue +28% over two years |
| Local organization building | Met | Two new sites opened; 310-employee organization |

### Evidence Highlights
- **E3:** €42M revenue scope and quantified EBITDA improvement
- **E3:** 9 direct reports / ~310 employees
- **E3:** Revenue growth and major-contract renegotiation outcomes
- **E3:** New-site openings demonstrating execution capability

### Strengths
1. Clear country-level ownership rather than narrow functional leadership
2. Strong commercial and operational balance
3. Quantified growth and profitability improvement
4. Significant organization scale and team leadership
5. Credible expansion and customer-management experience

### Risks / Verification Points
- Verify how independently the candidate sets strategy versus executing regional directives
- Clarify depth of regulatory, labor, and local-governance exposure
- Test whether performance improvements are sustainable and personally attributable

### Interview Focus
1. P&L decision making and trade-offs
2. Strategy versus regional alignment
3. Senior-team quality and succession
4. Customer concentration and commercial risk
5. Scaling systems, cadence, and governance

### Targeted Interview Questions
1. Which three P&L lines did you personally change the most, and how?
2. Tell me about a time you rejected or materially changed a regional strategy because it would not work locally.
3. How did you achieve the 28% revenue growth, and what portion came from pricing, new customers, or existing-account expansion?
4. Describe your weakest direct report two years ago. What did you do, and what happened?
5. What was the hardest part of opening the two new sites?
6. Which customer represented the greatest concentration risk, and how did you manage it?
7. What operating cadence do you use with your leadership team each week and month?

### Final Hiring View
Strongly recommend advancing. The candidate shows unusually complete evidence for a country-leadership role. Final interviews should focus on strategic independence, governance depth, and the repeatability of the reported commercial results.
